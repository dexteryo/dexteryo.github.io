§ 01

Verdict: two lanes to build, one to position for, one to avoid

The lanes are ranked against the real constraint set: passive-income fit, weekend maintainability, distance from the employer's business, and what a Vietnam-resident's US LLC can actually access in July 2026. Sections § 02–05 carry the evidence; § 06–08 carry the fund flows, the legal structure, and the employment analysis.

2 Ecommerce AI SaaS build · priced high Churn data kills cheap wrappers; $250+/mo B2B retains like normal SaaS. Few customers, high price.
3 Stablecoin / Open USD position Bridge carries custody and licence; Vietnam is in the payout footprint. But every headline feature is preview- or waitlist-gated, and OUSD has no builder APIs yet.

§ 02

Lane 1 — Agentic commerce: open standard, real volume, quarterly churn

The Agentic Commerce Protocol is an open, Apache 2.0-licensed standard with OpenAI and Stripe as founding maintainers (Meta credited later), announced 29 September 2025 alongside ChatGPT Instant Checkout, with a stated path to neutral foundation stewardship.1 A builder here is on a multi-company standard, not a proprietary Stripe API — though rival standards exist (Google AP2, Mastercard Agent Pay), and "first live standard" is Stripe's own framing.

The fund flow is the decisive property for a solo builder: the merchant stays merchant of record. Shared Payment Tokens pass between buyer, agent and business; the merchant confirms its own PaymentIntent; the agent layer never holds funds or raw card details.2 Tooling in this lane carries near-zero money-movement risk (drawn in DWG 06-02 and sequenced in DWG 06-05).

The cost side: ACP is beta, with five date-versioned breaking snapshots between September 2025 and April 2026 — roughly quarterly — and older versions deprecated.3 Live transactions already flow through Etsy and Shopify merchants, so adoption is real, but a weekend-only maintainer must budget for breaking-change windows.

Stripe's Agentic Commerce Suite (11 December 2025) bundles Product Discovery, Checkout Sessions, and Shared Payment Tokens with Radar. The claim that this forecloses third-party feed tooling was refuted 0–3: the middleware niche remains open.4

Product ideas analysis

  1. ACP conformance monitor — "is your shop still agent-buyable?" Continuous spec-version testing that alerts merchants when a quarterly snapshot breaks them. This converts the lane's biggest weakness into the retention mechanism: churn in the spec becomes the reason customers stay subscribed.
  2. Agent-readiness middleware — turn any catalogue (Shopify export, WooCommerce, bespoke) into a conformant ACP feed plus checkout endpoint, for merchants outside Stripe's own suite. Priced monthly per storefront.
  3. Vertical shopping agents — holds no funds, but competes with platform-native agents; the weakest of the three.
§ 03

Lane 2 — Ecommerce AI SaaS: the churn data dictates the shape

AI-native SaaS churns drastically worse than traditional B2B SaaS, and price point is the strongest predictor of survival. ChartMogul's billing data (~3,500 companies) puts AI-native median retention at 40% GRR / 48% NRR against 82% NRR for B2B SaaS — and products under $50/mo, the classic solo-builder wrapper price, retain just 23% of gross revenue annually. Above $250/mo, AI products retain essentially like normal B2B SaaS: 70% GRR / 85% NRR.5

Mobile data corroborates: AI apps keep 21.1% of annual subscribers at 12 months versus 30.7% for non-AI, yet convert trials 52% better and show higher realised LTV. The viable pattern is strong upfront monetisation with engineered retention — annual plans, workflow lock-in — not passive renewals on cheap monthly plans.6

The billing rails are first-party now: Stripe owns Metronome (used by OpenAI and Anthropic; enterprise, sales-led) and ships native Billing meters for tokens, API calls and agent workflows.7 Token billing adds automatic markup over raw OpenAI/Anthropic/Google model costs with auto-synced provider pricing — the resell-AI-compute-at-margin model as a product feature — but sits in private preview behind a waitlist.8

Shape analysis — target $250–500/mo, B2B, 20–80 customers: an AI operations tool embedded in a seller's weekly workflow (repricing, catalogue enrichment, support triage with escalation). Thirty customers at that price is roughly $9–15k MRR with retention that behaves like normal SaaS. The $9–29/mo wrapper tier is structurally incapable of passive income. No Shopify App Store revenue-distribution claim survived verification — treat "median app earns under $1k/mo" as plausible but uncited.

§ 04

Lane 3 — Stablecoin / Open USD: real rails, gated doors

The custody and licensing burden sits with Bridge (a Stripe company), not the builder. Stripe's docs state Bridge is custodian of stablecoin financial-account balances (USDC/USDB), and since 12 February 2026 Bridge holds OCC preliminary conditional approval for a national trust bank charter covering issuance, orchestration, reserve management and custody.9 A solo builder rides federally regulated infrastructure — with residual exposure: the account holder answers for its transactions, and moving third-party funds would trigger the builder's own money-transmission analysis. The approval is preliminary and revocable.

Vietnam is natively inside the stablecoin footprint — in the 102-country balance list and the 159-country crypto-wallet payout list — the only Stripe surface that touches Vietnam. That is fund-flow support, not account eligibility: the account holder remains the US LLC.10 The headline features are gated as of July 2026: stablecoin-backed Issuing cards (private preview; requires a US/Europe Connect platform), stablecoin-to-bank payouts (private preview), global payouts (waitlist). Nothing is self-serve.11

Bridge's Open Issuance (live since 30 September 2025) lets a business launch a branded stablecoin with Bridge as issuer of record, sharing the majority of reserve yield (~3–4% APY, BlackRock/Fidelity/Superstate-managed) minus a platform fee — proven by Phantom, MetaMask and Sui.12 It is the purest passive-income mechanism in this dossier, with three caveats: passing yield to coin holders would breach the GENIUS Act; one client (Hyperliquid) already sunset its coin; and KYB eligibility for a foreign-owned solo LLC is unverified.

Open Standard / OUSD — announced 30 June 2026 with 140+ backers (Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Adyen, Coinbase, Shopify) — launches "later in 2026" on Base, Ethereum, Solana and Tempo. No builder-facing APIs or economics exist; the claim that reserve yield flows to consortium partners was refuted.13 A watchlist item, not a buildable surface.

While gated analysis — get the LLC and Stripe account live on ungated products first (preview programmes onboard existing accounts faster); join both waitlists now; and hold the candidate product — USD invoicing with stablecoin settlement for SEA freelancers, own-funds only — until the payout gates open.

§ 05

Lane 4 — Affiliate / content: three verified blows in the entry window

Google AI Overviews now correlate with a 58% lower click-through rate for the top-ranking page (Ahrefs, 300k keywords, December 2023 → December 2025) — sharply worse than the 34.5% reduction measured in April 2025. 99.2% of AIO-triggering keywords are informational: exactly the affiliate traffic base. Independently corroborated by Pew (~47%), Seer (49–65%) and Authoritas (47.5%).14

Penske Media alleges a one-third drop in affiliate revenue by late 2024 in its federal suit against Google; MailOnline reported a 56% desktop CTR drop in its CMA submission. Both are contested claims, not adjudicated fact — but if maximal-authority publishers lose a third of affiliate revenue, a new solo site entering 2026 faces worse odds.15

Amazon quietly cut Associates commissions by as much as 50%, rolling through Asia-Pacific in late 2025 and reaching the US around 9 March 2026; premium categories fell from ~10% to 4–5% and milestone bonuses were eliminated.16 The one open angle — affiliate feeds for AI shopping agents — produced no verified evidence in either direction: an unknown, not an opportunity. Stripe plays no structural role in affiliate income, which also makes this the least Stripe-native lane.

§ 07

Legal structure — the Vietnam ↔ US skeleton, piece by piece

US LLC formation is legally open to Vietnam residents; the real obligations are an annual Form 5472 attached to a pro-forma 1120 (a $25,000 penalty for failure), a US registered agent, and an EIN via the slower non-SSN route.17 Stripe Atlas's "bank account and payments within two business days" marketing was refuted 0–3 — plan in weeks.18

At 183+ days in-country you are a Vietnam tax resident taxed on worldwide income, LLC profits included; the 2026 PIT reform changed brackets, not residency.19 Decree 103/2026/ND-CP (effective 3 April 2026) exempts outbound investments under VND 7 billion (~US$270k) in non-conditional sectors from the Offshore Investment Registration Certificate — a solo software LLC instead needs SBV foreign-exchange registration, a National Investment Information System declaration, periodic reporting, and repatriation of distributed profits within 12 months through the registered account. The statutory trigger is Vietnamese nationality, not residency, and the regime is three months old — SBV branch practice is untested.20

Mercury explicitly prohibits founders living in Vietnam — the test is residence, not incorporation, so the LLC does not cure it.21 No claim about alternatives (Relay, Wise Business, Brex, Atlas-partner banks) survived verification: validate directly, before forming the LLC. Vietnam's Digital Technology Industry Law (crypto assets legal from 1 January 2026) also produced no verified claims on holding USD stablecoins from foreign business income — unresearched, not settled.

§ 08

Employment conflict — the contract is the decisive document

Under Irish law, side-project ownership turns almost entirely on the employment contract and the "course of employment" test, not on a sweeping statutory grab. CRRA 2000 s.23(1) makes the author first owner of copyright unless the work is made in the course of employment — and even that yields to "any agreement to the contrary". The Patents Act 1992 s.16(1) is only a choice-of-law rule; employee-invention ownership falls to Irish common law plus the contract.22 A genuine side project built outside employment defaults to the author. The refuted companion claim matters too: there is no clean "inventor owns by default" rule for employees — the common-law course-of-employment test fills the gap.23

What follows is proximity analysis, not verified law: the employer's platform is Stripe Connect + Issuing for business card programmes — connected-account KYB onboarding, card issuance with spending controls, funding transfers, webhook mirroring into a banking core, expense-card products for European businesses. Distance from that stack is the safety variable.

ACP merchant tooling; ecommerce AI SaaS; affiliate / content distant Different buyers, different problem space, no overlap with card issuing or business payments.

Hygiene checklist analysis — in order, because each step gates the next:

  1. Read the contract clauses on IP-assignment scope, the "course of employment" definition, exclusivity/moonlighting, and non-compete — before writing code.
  2. Personal hardware, personal accounts, personal time only. Never employer laptops, repositories, snippets, or internal documents.
  3. Keep the side project entirely out of employer systems.
  4. If the assignment clause reads "any IP created during employment" rather than "in the course of employment", get written clarification or consent before building anything fintech-shaped.
  5. Never solicit employer customers or use non-public employer knowledge (fraud thresholds, vendor terms, roadmap).
