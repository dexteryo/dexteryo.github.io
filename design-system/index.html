§ 01

Principles

Evidence over decoration. Every rule, colour, and label must encode something true about the content. Provenance — who verified what, when, against which source — is a first-class visual element, not a footnote.1

The accent means verified. There is one accent colour, a deep viridian, and it is the same colour as the real-money edge in diagrams and the ok status. Green is never decoration; when you see it, something is real or confirmed.

Two modes, shared tokens. Documents are read; panels are scanned. They share one palette, one spacing scale, and one set of semantics, so a report and its dashboard look like siblings.

Portability is a hard constraint. System font stacks only, no webfonts, no external resources. Output must survive a sandboxed artifact viewer, an email attachment, a ticket, and a printer.

1. Inspired by the engineering title block: a drawing without a revision date and an approver is not a drawing, it is a sketch.

§ 03

Document mode

For investigations, design docs, post-mortems, and wiki exports. Serif body at 17px, prose capped at 66ch, and citations pushed to the margin so the argument stays clean. Everything below is live — the demo content is a fictional payments platform.

The FundWallet step is the only automated movement of real money in the estate: a single transfer from the platform float to the customer wallet.2 Every other record — the authorisation ledger, the double-entry journal — is a mirror. Mirrors can drift; the transfer cannot.

The one-way valve: money enters through five rails and leaves through one. The ledgers record a symmetry the money does not have.

§ 04

Panel mode

For dashboards, risk registers, and status pages. Sans-serif, 1px borders instead of shadows, status as dots, machine values in monospace with tabular numerals. Light-first so it prints and screenshots cleanly; the dark theme comes free from the tokens.

§ 05

Diagram grammar

The grammar is semantic and skin-independent: a restyle may recolour it but may never reinterpret it. Every diagram ships with the legend and a title block carrying revision date and verification status.

Zoom levels follow the C4 model: Context (whole estate) → Container (services) → Component (inside one service). One diagram, one level — never mix.

This grammar is Tier 1, for architecture and money-flow diagrams. All other diagram types — sequence, state, class, ER, gantt, and the rest of the Mermaid catalogue — are Tier 2: Mermaid DSL rendered with the DDS theme bridge. See the diagrams reference for the tier rules and live examples.
