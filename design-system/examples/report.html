The drift is in the mirror, not the money

§ 01

What was compared

The platform keeps three books. The float is real money at the payment provider; the wallet ledger is the balance the product shows; the journal is the double-entry record the accountants close against. Only the float can move money — the other two are mirrors of it.1

The nightly job compares all three per wallet. On 2026-07-02 it reported 248 wallets where the wallet ledger disagreed with both other books — the first divergence since the job was introduced.

1. recon_job.sql:41 — three-way join keyed on wallet ID, run nightly at 03:00 UTC.
§ 02

Where the books disagree

Every divergent wallet was funded between 02:10 and 02:14 UTC, the window in which the funding-events consumer restarted and replayed its in-flight batch. The replay applied 248 credits twice to the wallet ledger; the float and the journal, which are keyed on the provider's transfer ID, deduplicated correctly.2

2. FundingConsumer.java:88 — offset commit happens after apply, not atomically with it; a restart between the two replays the batch.

§ 03

Why no customer saw wrong money

Spending authorises against the float, not the wallet ledger, so the doubled mirror could over-display but never over-spend. The blast radius is cosmetic — 248 customers briefly saw a balance up to €41.20 too high — plus the reconciliation alarm itself.

The invariant held: mirrors may drift; only the book that moves money must be right by construction.

Make the wallet ledger idempotent on the same key as its siblings.

The other two books survived the replay because they deduplicate on the provider transfer ID. The fix is symmetry, not cleverness.

  1. Add a unique constraint on (wallet ID, transfer ID) to the wallet ledger and upsert on apply.
  2. Replay the 02:10–02:14 window through the fixed path; confirm the 248 gaps close.
  3. Keep the nightly three-way job — it caught this within one day.
