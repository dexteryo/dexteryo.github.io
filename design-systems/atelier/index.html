№ 01

Principles

Lightness is the luxury. A display face set at 300 says the argument does not need to raise its voice; a bold headline is a system shouting to be believed. Atelier earns financial-grade trust the way a good magazine does — confident type, generous margins, and every claim answered by a citation.1

Whitespace is the structure. Sections are separated by space and a folio number, not by rules or boxes. Where a line is needed it is a hairline; the double rule appears only where print tradition put it — under the masthead, over the colophon, closing a sum.

One accent, spent like ink. Oxblood is the printer's second colour: the drop cap, the hanging quotation mark, one emphasised word, the verdict label. It is never a background and never decoration.

Evidence closes the section. Claims carry superscript numbers and each section ends with a footnote strip under the short printer's rule — monospace citations, verification dates. Not margin sidenotes; those belong to DDS.

1. The lineage: Stripe Press, The Paris Review, FT Weekend, Economist print charts, Tschichold's Penguin composition rules. README.md — Lineage

№ 04

Panel mode

The Economist/FT print-data idiom: the same warm paper, hairline horizontal rules, no cards and no boxes. Numbers are very large and very light; statuses are solid squares in the semantic ink; chips are small-caps text with a thin coloured underline.

№ 05

Figures

Charts and diagrams sit between hairlines like a plate in a book, captioned in small caps and numbered ("Fig. № 1"). Charts are inline SVG using the viz palette in order; sequence, state, and ER diagrams are Mermaid pre-rendered to static SVG with mermaid-config.json.

Atelier has no native diagram grammar; anything that needs the money/mirror/blocked edge semantics is a DDS job — use the Dexter Design System for it.
